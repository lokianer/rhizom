# Über die Wurzeln

"Über die Wurzeln" is German and means "on the roots" or "about the roots", with the same ambiguity as the English *on*: a treatise about roots, or something lying on top of them. It is what somebody pencilled in the margin of my second-hand German edition of *A Thousand Plateaus*, next to the paragraph where Deleuze and Guattari announce that they are tired of trees. I have no idea whether the previous owner meant it as a summary, a joke or the title of a paper they never wrote, and I have kept the note because all three readings are useful.

## As a summary

The rhizome chapter is *about roots* in the sense of being against them: against the root-book, the single origin, the trunk from which everything descends. See [[Rhizomes#The six principles]].

## As a joke

The chapter is a thousand-plateau book lying *on top of* the roots it claims to have done away with. It has a title, chapters, page numbers and an index. So does this vault. See [[Rhizomes#Where the metaphor stops]].

## As a title

The paper I would write, if I wrote papers, would be about the actual roots: what grows between plants underground, how it behaves like a network and how it does not. That note exists; it is [[Mycorrhizal networks]].
